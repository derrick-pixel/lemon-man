The problem

A market that cannot tell good labour from bad.

Economist George Akerlof named it the market for lemons: when buyers cannot distinguish quality before purchase, the whole market degrades. Singapore's shift-labour market runs on exactly that asymmetry.

An employer cannot tell a reliable worker from one with a pattern of no-shows, unsupported medical leave, or data-handling breaches — until the shift is already missed and the client contract is already at risk.

"Bad actors can freely re-enter. A worker dismissed at one agency simply reappears at another, under the same identity, with no record following them." — Business Plan §2.1

01 / asymmetry

One side gets reviewed. One side doesn't.

Workers can rate any employer on Glassdoor, Google, JobStreet and TikTok. Employers have no comparable channel — and no neutral one that crosses agencies.

02 / cost

The cost of failure is asymmetric too.

One no-show in F&B costs S$200–1,000 in wages, backfill premium and liquidated damages. In security, healthcare or aviation, a missed shift can void an entire contract.

03 / precedent

Finance already solved this.

Since 2023, MAS has required financial institutions to run structured reference checks on "rolling bad apples". Every other sector was left without the infrastructure.

Not a blacklist — a utility

A reliability network, the way a credit bureau is a credit network.

A one-sided adverse-record database is not viable in Singapore — it collides with the PDPA, defamation law, and the Workplace Fairness Act. Lemon Man is built as the labour-conduct analogue of a credit bureau: structured, bidirectional, and defensible by design.

Bidirectional

Both sides on record

Employers contribute conduct records; workers claim profiles, gather endorsements and file counter-records against employers.

Evidence-first

No evidence, no record

Every adverse record uses a fixed factual schema with mandatory evidence. Free-text "attitude" fields are rejected at intake.

Time-bound

Records expire

Adverse records decay over 12–60 months by severity. Positive endorsements persist. There is no pay-to-remove.

Right of reply

The worker is notified first

Every adverse record carries a 21-day reply window before it is ever visible. Disputed records are hidden until review concludes.

How it works

Three moves, one shared record.

An employer files a structured record

A no-show, a late cancellation, a verified data-secrecy breach — submitted against a fixed schema, with evidence attached and mitigating context declared. No free-text allegations.

The worker is notified and can reply

The worker has 21 days to respond before anything is published. They can dispute with evidence — and a disputed record stays hidden until the review concludes.

A reliability score follows the worker

Verified employers see a 0–1000 score: adverse records weighted by severity and recency, positive endorsements, tenure and training. Thin profiles read "insufficient data" — never a spurious score.

The full mechanism →

What MAS made mandatory for banks in 2023 — structured reference checks, a five-year lookback, a fixed schema and a right of reply — Lemon Man makes workable for everyone else.

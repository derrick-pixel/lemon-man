Trust & law

Built to be defensible.

A workforce reliability network sits at the intersection of the PDPA, defamation law and the Workplace Fairness Act. Lemon Man treats that as the design brief — not a problem to be papered over later.

The precedent

MAS already proved the model — for banks.

Since December 2023, the Monetary Authority of Singapore has required financial institutions to run structured reference checks on "rolling bad apples" — staff whose misconduct would otherwise follow them quietly from firm to firm.

Lemon Man borrows that architecture wholesale and extends it to the shift, contract and freelance sectors the regulation never reached. The MAS regime is our design template — and the clearest public answer to the question "is this kind of data-sharing legitimate in Singapore?"

What the MAS regime established

Mandatory structured reference checks on hire
A 21-day mandatory response window
A bounded five-year misconduct lookback
A fixed schema of disclosable information categories
The individual's right to view their own reference
A reasonable-care standard, consistent with case law

The MAS framework is a precedent and a model — not a statutory shield. Lemon Man earns its defensibility through architecture, not by borrowing someone else's immunity.

The architecture

Four bodies of law. Four answers, in the design.

PDPA

Personal Data Protection Act

Claimed profiles run on worker consent via Singpass. Employer lookups rely on the evaluative-purpose exception and are logged. A statutory DPO, worker notification within the reply window, rights of access and correction, and hard-coded retention limits are built in — not bolted on.

Defamation

The Ramesh Krishnan standard

Singapore's Court of Appeal requires reasonable care, no cherry-picking, and a real chance to respond before adverse information is used. Lemon Man answers with a facts-only schema, mandatory evidence, a mandatory mitigating-context field, and a reply window before publication.

WFA

Workplace Fairness Act

Records alleging protected characteristics — or anything constructable as discriminatory — are rejected at intake. Disciplinary records require evidence of due inquiry. An annual transparency report covers records submitted, disputed, upheld and expired.

Symmetry

Structural bidirectionality

Workers and employers are both data subjects, both contributors, both with rights of reply. That symmetry is the strongest single defence — it makes the platform impossible to characterise as a one-sided instrument against workers.

The hard line

What Lemon Man will never build.

Some features would generate revenue and destroy the platform's legitimacy at the same time. They are excluded by policy, in writing, permanently.

✕

No pay-to-remove

A record cannot be bought off. It expires on its schedule, or through rehabilitation — never through payment.

✕

No blacklist framing

No "blacklist", no "bad-apple list", no mutual-assured-destruction language — in product, marketing, or internal use.

✕

No back-channel removal

No off-platform worker-employer negotiation to delete records. That is coercion, and it is out of bounds.

✕

No offshore evasion

Singapore data residency at launch. No hosting arrangement designed to put the platform beyond the reach of a regulator.

✕

No free-text "attitude"

No subjective culture-fit or attitude fields. Facts against a closed schema, or nothing.

✕

No unevidenced flags

No "do not rehire" marker that is not backed by a structured, evidenced record in the schema.

Counsel

Two parallel legal opinions

The architecture is being put to two independent Singapore law firms in parallel before any platform is built — and modified to whatever they jointly require.

Oversight

External DPO & regulator dialogue

An external Data Protection Officer from day one, with informal pre-consultation sought from the PDPC, MOM and TAFEP before launch.

Cover

S$5M insurance from launch

Professional indemnity, cyber and media-liability cover is bound before the first record is ever filed — not after the first claim.

The schema is the legal product. A record that cannot be defended — evidenced, contextualised, and answered — is a record Lemon Man will not store.

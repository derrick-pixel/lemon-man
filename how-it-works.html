The mechanism

How a record becomes a score.

Three things make Lemon Man defensible where a blacklist would not be: a fixed factual schema, a notification window before anything is published, and a score that decays. Here is the full path.

01 — The structured record

No free text. A fixed factual schema.

Lemon Man never stores an opinion. Every adverse record is a set of facts against a closed schema — and a record missing any mandatory field is rejected before it is ever stored.

02 — The reply window

The worker is told before anyone else.

A blacklist publishes first and argues later. Lemon Man inverts that. A record is held — invisible to every employer — until the worker has had a full window to reply.

Filed & held

The moment an employer submits a record, the worker is notified — substance of the record, the contributing employer, and the dispute process. The record is not visible to anyone during this period.

21 days to reply

The worker has 21 days — matching the MAS reference-check standard — to accept, add context, or formally dispute with their own evidence.

Disputed records stay hidden

If a record is disputed, it remains hidden — not merely flagged — until an independent review concludes. A contested record never causes real-time economic harm.

Published — or expired

Only an undisputed or review-upheld record becomes visible to verified employers. From that point its decay clock runs; on expiry it is purged.

03 — The scoring engine

A 0–1000 reliability score, credit-bureau style.

A verified employer never sees raw allegations dumped on a screen. They see a single score, computed from four weighted inputs — and a confidence band that refuses to guess.

  • ◆ Adverse records — weighted by severity, recency, corroboration across employers, and dispute status.
  • ◆ Positive endorsements — weighted by issuer credibility and recency.
  • ◆ Tenure & shift count — raw experience on record.
  • ◆ Training certifications — WSQ and MOM-recognised credentials.

A profile with fewer than three records returns "insufficient data" — never a spurious number. Thin data is the single largest defamation risk, so the engine declines to score it.

Decay schedule

Nothing is permanent.

Severity sets the lookback. The most serious category mirrors the MAS five-year window; the lightest clears in a year. A worker's past does not follow them forever.

Record categoryBehavioural basisLookback
No-show / late cancellationReliability lapse, low corroboration burden12 months

A structured rehabilitation pathway — WSQ training, fresh endorsements — can accelerate the expiry of adverse records ahead of schedule.
